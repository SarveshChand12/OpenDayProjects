🌩️ The basics

So what actually is cloud computing?

Cloud computing means using computer storage, processing power, and software that lives on someone else's servers — accessed over the internet — instead of buying and running your own equipment.

Think of it like electricity ⚡

You don't own a power station in your backyard. You just plug into the grid and pay for what you use. Cloud computing works the same way, except instead of electricity, you're plugging into storage space and computing power.

🧱 Three flavours

SaaS, PaaS, IaaS — the alphabet soup, decoded

Cloud services come in three main "layers," depending on how much control you get vs. how much the provider handles for you.

SaaS

Software as a Service

A finished app you just open and use. No installing, no setup — someone else runs everything behind the scenes.

📌 Examples: Gmail, Netflix, Spotify
PaaS

Platform as a Service

A ready-made toolkit developers use to build and launch their own apps, without worrying about the servers underneath.

📌 Examples: Google App Engine, Heroku — the platforms devs build apps on top of
IaaS

Infrastructure as a Service

Raw computing power — virtual servers, storage, networking — rented by the hour. You control everything built on top.

📌 Example: Amazon Web Services EC2 — literally renting a computer in a data center

How does Fiji connect to all this?

"The cloud" isn't floating above us — it's real buildings full of humming server racks, usually in places like the US, Australia, or Singapore. Pacific nations like Fiji reach that global infrastructure through submarine internet cables — massive fiber-optic cables laid across the ocean floor connecting our islands to the rest of the world's internet backbone.

🇫🇯 Real scenario: a small shop in Suva

A local handicraft business wants to sell online. Instead of buying and maintaining its own servers (expensive, and someone has to manage them), it uses cloud-based tools to launch a store in a weekend — payments, hosting, and storage all rented from a cloud provider. It only pays for what it uses, and can handle a flood of orders during tourist season without crashing.

🔐 Privacy check

Wait — who can actually see my stuff?

Fair question. Your data on a cloud service is encrypted and access-controlled, but security is a team effort between you and the provider — this is called the shared responsibility model.

☁️ The provider handles

  • Physical security of data centers
  • Keeping servers running & patched
  • Protecting the underlying network

🧑 You handle

  • Using a strong, unique password
  • Turning on two-factor authentication
  • Not sharing your login with randoms

It's not scary — it's just teamwork. The provider locks the building; you lock your own door.
