From mainframes to your pocket — how did "the cloud" get this big?

Quick recap in case you're landing here first: cloud computing means using storage, processing power, and software that live on someone else's servers, accessed over the internet, instead of running your own equipment. This page is about two things — where it's actually being used today, and the surprisingly long history behind how we got here.

🌍 Bigger than you think

Where is cloud computing actually used?

Almost every industry you can name now runs some part of itself in the cloud.

Media

Entertainment & streaming

Netflix, Spotify, and YouTube all serve their content straight out of cloud data centers.

Finance

Banking & finance

Many banks run core systems and mobile banking apps on cloud infrastructure for security and uptime.

Health

Healthcare

Patient records, telehealth video visits, and medical research increasingly run in the cloud.

Education

Online learning

School and university platforms — video lessons, assignment portals, learning systems — are usually cloud-hosted.

Agriculture

Agriculture & fisheries

Satellite and sensor data for farming and fishing fleets gets processed and stored in the cloud.

Government

Government services

Many government services — tax filing, ID systems, public records — run on cloud platforms for cost and reliability.

Gaming

Gaming

Cloud gaming and multiplayer servers rely on cloud infrastructure to handle millions of simultaneous players.

Emergency

Disaster response

During cyclones and natural disasters, cloud systems help coordinate emergency communication and relief efforts.

🇫🇯 Closer to home

Cloud computing in Fiji & the Pacific

This isn't just a Silicon Valley story — cloud computing is already part of daily life across the region.

🎓

Schools & universities

Regional institutions use cloud-based learning platforms so students across many islands and countries can access the same courses.

🛍️

Small businesses

Local businesses increasingly use cloud tools for online stores, payments, and accounting instead of buying their own servers.

🏛️

Government services

Cloud-based systems help back up critical records and deliver digital services more reliably.

🌪️

Disaster resilience

Cloud backups mean records and services can keep running even if local infrastructure is damaged during cyclone season.

💡 Why the cloud makes sense for smaller nations

Building and running your own data center is expensive and needs constant maintenance. For a country the size of Fiji, renting cloud infrastructure from a global provider is often far more practical than owning it outright — the same idea covered on the Cloud Computing basics page.
